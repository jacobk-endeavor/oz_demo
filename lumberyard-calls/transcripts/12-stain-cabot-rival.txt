# Title: Stain, grain pop, and the wrong aisle at the other store
# Rep: Pilar Duarte — coatings counter
# Customer: Hector Ruiz — cabinet maker on a small deck side job
# Notable: Behr at Home Depot as competitor; customer prefers Cabot clear toner on this cedar; buying stain and brushes
# Tags: competitor, product-preference, purchase

Pilar [rep]: Hector, you are matching an existing handrail that looks like a natural cedar that caught sun for two seasons.
Hector [customer]: I tried a no-name at Home Depot that went orange in a week, and a Behr can that the desk swore was “exterior” but it sat on the surface like plastic.
Pilar [rep]: On cedar I would rather sell you a penetrating system that is meant for decks, and I will show you a wet sample on a scrap so you are not painting blind.
Hector [customer]: I have always been comfortable with a Cabot clear toner for this spec because it does not go chalky, and the grain still reads like wood.
Pilar [rep]: I can pair the toner with a deck wash for prep, a pad applicator, and a brush you will not throw away after one run.
Hector [customer]: I will buy the whole kit, and add two canvas drop cloths because the client has pavers that stain if you look at them wrong.
Pilar [rep]: I will hand you a stir stick that has not been used for who-knows-what, because cross-contaminated stain is a bad surprise on a nice rail.
